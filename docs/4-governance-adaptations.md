# 04 – Governance Adaptations

Lightweight patterns inspired by AIC that non-profits may consider.

These are suggestions for reflection and experimentation, not prescriptions.

## 1. Decision Log (inspired by AIC-Archive spirit)

Maintain a simple, append-only record of significant decisions:

- Date
- Decision summary
- Options considered
- Key reasons for the chosen path
- Who was involved
- Any dissent or unresolved concerns
- Review date (if applicable)

This creates institutional memory and makes patterns of influence more visible over time.

## 2. Human-Gating Points

Identify categories of decisions that should not be fully automated or delegated without explicit human confirmation:

- Acceptance of major funding or partnerships
- Changes to core mission language
- Public political or advocacy positions
- Significant shifts in program portfolio
- Handling of sensitive personal or community data

## 3. Periodic Independence Review

At regular intervals (e.g. annually), ask:

- Which relationships currently give external parties the most influence over our decisions?
- Have any of those relationships begun to shape our priorities in ways we did not originally intend?
- What would it cost us (financially and otherwise) to walk away from our largest sources of support?

## 4. Separation of Proposal and Authorization

Where possible, separate the role of developing a proposal from the role of finally authorizing it.  
This is a soft version of the spirit behind Ethical Kernel checks: create a moment where the organization can still say no.

## 5. Public vs Internal Transparency

Not everything needs to be public.  
But significant governance processes and the principles guiding them benefit from being explainable to members, beneficiaries, and serious observers.
