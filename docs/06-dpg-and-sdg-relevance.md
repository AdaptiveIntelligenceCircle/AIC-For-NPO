# 06 – Relevance to Digital Public Goods and SDGs

**Status:** Draft statement – November 2026  
**Tone:** Under-claim

---

## 1. Context

Adaptive Intelligence Circle (AIC) has submitted materials in connection with the Digital Public Goods Alliance (DPGA) review process.  

This document explains, in limited and careful terms, how **AIC-For-NPO** relates to the ideas of Digital Public Goods (DPGs) and the Sustainable Development Goals (SDGs).

This repository is **not** itself a certified Digital Public Good.  
Any broader recognition depends on the outcome of the DPGA process and on future development of the core AIC protocol family.

---

## 2. What AIC-For-NPO attempts to contribute

AIC-For-NPO explores whether certain design principles can help non-profit organizations:

- Maintain clearer independence from capture (by large funders, political pressure, or commercial interests)
- Keep human judgment central in important decisions
- Increase internal transparency of significant decisions
- Reduce the risk that AI or coordination tools hollow out organizational purpose

These concerns intersect with several themes that appear in discussions of Digital Public Goods and responsible digital public infrastructure:

- Openness and inspectability
- Avoidance of harm and unintended power concentration
- Support for institutions that serve public or community purposes
- Long-term stewardship rather than short-term extraction

---

## 3. Relation to SDGs (modest mapping)

AIC-For-NPO does not claim direct, measurable contribution to specific SDG targets at this stage.  

At a high level, the work is conceptually adjacent to:

- **SDG 16** (Peace, Justice and Strong Institutions) — through emphasis on transparent decision processes and resistance to capture
- **SDG 9** (Industry, Innovation and Infrastructure) — through exploration of ethical, open coordination infrastructure
- **SDG 17** (Partnerships) — through an attempt to offer principles that independent organizations can adapt without creating new dependencies

These are directional affinities only. They are not performance claims.

---

## 4. Do No Harm considerations

Consistent with the spirit of the DPG Standard’s “do no harm” expectation, AIC-For-NPO adopts the following constraints:

- It must not facilitate hidden control by external funders or political actors.
- It must not encourage the replacement of human ethical responsibility with automated systems.
- It must not present itself as a turnkey solution that reduces an organization’s own accountability.
- Documentation and future tools should remain open to scrutiny.

---

## 5. Limitations (explicit)

- This repository is still in early design / documentation stage.
- No formal impact evaluation has been conducted.
- No claim is made that adoption of these ideas automatically improves governance outcomes.
- Real-world value depends entirely on how organizations choose to interpret and apply the principles, and on the future maturity of Parallel TestNet components.

---

## 6. Summary position

AIC-For-NPO is a modest, principle-oriented adaptation layer.  
It aims to be compatible with the values often associated with Digital Public Goods — openness, reduced harm, and support for independent institutions — while refusing to over-state its current readiness or impact.

Future alignment with DPG criteria will depend on the evolution of the broader AIC ecosystem and on external review processes.
