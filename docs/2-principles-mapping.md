# 02 – Principles Mapping

How core AIC principles can be interpreted in a non-profit organizational context.

## 1. Ethical-from-kernel

**In AIC:** Ethical constraints and refusal mechanisms sit close to authorization, not only as after-the-fact policy.

**In an NPO context:**
- Important decisions (funding acceptance, partnerships, major program changes, public statements) should have explicit evaluation points before they are finalized.
- “Fail-closed” attitude: when in doubt about alignment with mission and independence, the default is to pause or refuse rather than proceed and justify later.

## 2. Third Path

**In AIC:** Refusal of pure state capture *and* pure commercial rule-power over the protocol.

**In an NPO context:**
- Avoid structures that make the organization highly dependent on a single large funder, government program, or commercial partner.
- Prefer diversified, mission-aligned support and clear boundaries on what external parties can influence.
- Regularly examine whether current relationships are quietly shifting decision-making power away from the organization’s stated purpose.

## 3. Zero-donation (spirit)

**In AIC:** Strong stance against funding that creates political, commercial, personal, or institutional capture.

**In an NPO context:**
- Not a ban on all external funding.
- A discipline of asking: “Does this support preserve our ability to say no? Does it create hidden obligations or personal benefits that distort judgment?”
- Transparency about funding sources and conditions is treated as a governance necessity, not optional public relations.

## 4. Human Meaning

**In AIC:** Meaning-critical acts should remain human-gated; technology should not hollow out human purpose.

**In an NPO context:**
- Protect time and process for genuine human deliberation on questions of purpose, ethics, and long-term direction.
- Resist the pressure to let metrics, dashboards, or automated recommendations replace judgment on mission-critical issues.
- Treat “efficiency” as secondary when it conflicts with the organization’s reason for existing.

## 5. Transparency & Under-claim

- Maintain inspectable records of significant decisions.
- Avoid over-stating what tools or frameworks can achieve.
- Prefer clear limitations over impressive-sounding claims.
